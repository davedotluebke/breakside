Commentator voice mockups

Four ways the replay viewer could speak a game, on the same three-point sample (Breakside vs. Riverside, our names only). Every track was built one log entry at a time, exactly as the controller's entry hook would deliver them, with a 450 ms gap between clips to stand in for play-after-play pacing.

Approaches A and B differ in what is said. B, C and D say the same commentator script and differ in who says it. E uses no server at all. All Amazon Polly voices were synthesized from this session with the deploy credentials; the two candidates that need keys we do not hold here are listed at the end.

A

Verbatim log lines

Polly generative · Matthew

The baseline: the entry text read as written, with only the parentheses and dashes tidied. No model call in the loop, so it is the cheapest and simplest pipeline.

Listen for the stiffness. "Breakside on offense" and "Current score" lines are log furniture, not commentary, and the voice cannot know a block is exciting.

Per-line text and latency
B

Commentator rewrite

Polly generative · Stephen

Each entry rewritten into a commentator's line, using the entries already spoken for context (plan Decision 12). Possession delimiters go silent; roster lines become "On the line for Breakside"; a block becomes "Bob gets a hand on it!".

The rewrite here was written by hand to show the ceiling. In the product it is one short model call per entry, which adds its own latency on top of the synthesis numbers below.

C

Rewrite plus SSML emphasis

Polly neural · Matthew

Same script as B on the older neural engine, which accepts SSML the generative engine refuses: calm lines use the newscaster style, and blocks, turnovers and goals (green edge in the table) get a faster, louder read with a pause at every ellipsis.

Fastest and about half the price of generative. The trade is a flatter, more synthetic timbre; judge whether the emphasis control earns that back.

Per-line text and latency
D

Rewrite, long-form voice

Polly long-form · Ruth

Polly's long-form engine is tuned for narration and audiobooks: the most natural phrasing of the four and the only female voice in the set. It is also the slowest per call and over three times the price of generative.

Its strength is sustained reading, which a play-by-play of two-second lines never gives it. Included so the quality ceiling of Polly is on the table.

E

Your browser's own voice

Web Speech API · no server, no cost
Uses whatever English voice this device offers. Quality varies a lot: iOS Safari and macOS ship good ones, Android and Windows are more mixed.

The zero-cost control. Works offline, which fits Breakside's offline-first design, and needs nothing on the backend. The voice is whatever the device has, so coaches on different phones hear different things and we cannot tune it.

A reasonable fallback tier: if a Polly fetch fails or the viewer is offline, speak the line this way rather than go silent.

Side by side

Latency is the wall-clock time of one synthesis call from this container to Polly in us-east-1, per line, over the lines above. Cost per game assumes about 180 spoken entries at the commentator script's average line length (roughly 8,000 characters), at Polly's published per-character list prices; the rewrite model call is extra and small.

Recommendation

  1. Ship the rewrite, not the raw lines. A and B make the clearest difference of anything here. The voice engine matters less than the words.
  2. Start with Polly generative (B). It is already inside the AWS account the app deploys to, costs cents per game, and its latency fits play-after-play if the next line is synthesized while the current one plays. The IAM policy needs one polly:SynthesizeSpeech grant.
  3. Keep E as the fallback for offline viewers and failed fetches; the hook already caps how long replay waits for audio.
  4. Revisit C only if emphasis control proves necessary. The generative engine honours punctuation and exclamation marks well enough that the SSML gain is modest.
  5. If the ceiling matters more than cost, the two candidates below are the ones to render next.

Not rendered here

OpenAI gpt-4o-mini-tts takes a free-text style instruction ("an excited ultimate frisbee play-by-play announcer") and is widely rated the most natural low-latency option. The backend already holds an OpenAI key for narration, so a sample needs one server-side call. The key does not reach this container.

ElevenLabs remains the quality benchmark for character voices and supports streaming; it would be a new vendor, a new key and the highest per-character price of the set.
