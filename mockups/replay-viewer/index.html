What this shows

A Replay view: the Field tab's pitch with the lineup strip, the game log below, one transport bar between them. The disc travels from spot to spot; players step onto the field when an event places them. The log is the scrubber: tap any line to jump there. The timeline shows one bar per point (blue = we scored, red = they scored, orange = in progress).

The slider runs Live · 1× · 2× · 4× · Play after play. Live follows the tail: new events animate as they sync in; stepping back drops you to 1× and a "Go live" pill appears (try "Simulate a live event" both ways). Play after play ignores game time and fires each event as soon as the previous animation (and narration, when on) finishes.

Pacing otherwise follows the recorded timestamps divided by the speed, with dead time clipped by the two caps in the controls above. Point 4 has no timestamps and no field positions: its events play play-after-play regardless of the slider and the field collapses to a banner, which is how every game recorded before this feature will look.

Edit (paused only) unlocks dragging the disc or the holder to move the last spot, and a pencil on each log line opens the edit sheet (location, players, modifiers in v1).

Narration uses the browser's built-in voice as a stand-in for the future commentator; in play-after-play mode the next event waits for the sentence to finish, which is how the real TTS will gate pacing.

Decisions (2026-09-03)

  1. Timestamps. Every new event gets an at; older games are never re-timed — untimed events play play-after-play.
  2. Players. Only events place players; the strip shows the whole line.
  3. Rendering reuses the Field tab: pitch, markers, and the fade of older plays.
  4. Where. Log tab first, then post-game summary, then the share viewer. The Log tab is unchanged for games without positions.
  5. Dead time. 4 s within a point, 8 s between points, each with Off, in Advanced Settings.
  6. Orientation. User's pick: landscape (default) or portrait; the Log tab itself stays portrait on the phone.
  7. Editing v1. Location, players, modifiers. A receiver change asks whether to fix the next thrower or insert two Unknown Player passes.
  8. Commentator. Events stand alone; each prompt carries what has already been said.
